# Dice (SPEC §6.5)

Owner's requirement (2026-10-07): an animated dice roller like D&D Beyond's, as long as it looks exactly the same to the player. D&D Beyond lets the physics in the browser decide the roll; here **the server decides** and the dice are animated to land on its numbers, which looks the same, is evenly random, and can't be faked from the page (that matters once rolls are shared).

- **Rolling:** `POST /campaigns/:cid/roll` (anyone in the campaign) with notation (`1d20+5`, `2d6+1d4-1`, `d%`; d2, d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20, d100; up to 50 dice) and a mode. Advantage/disadvantage applies when the roll has exactly one d20: it's rolled twice and the higher/lower kept. Numbers come from `crypto.randomInt`. Parsing and the arithmetic are in `shared/src/dice.js` (the page uses the same file to read dice out of an attack's damage, e.g. `1d8+2 piercing`). Who sees a roll: "Sharing" below.
- **3D:** the dice are 3D and really thrown (physics), then their faces are relabelled so the face that lands up shows the server's number. A d100 is a tens die and a units die. Dice take the theme's accent colours. Off with reduced motion or when switched off in the tray; then the result just appears.
- **Rollers** (owner's call, 2026-10-08; Quick, Lite, Flat and None were removed). `DICE_ROLLER` in `.env` picks the default for everyone (`deluxe` unless set; sent to the page in `GET /me` as `dice.roller`); each player can pick the other in the tray (kept per browser; a saved choice that no longer exists falls back to the server's). `ROLLERS` in `dice.js`:
  - `deluxe` (default; owner's request, 2026-10-07): `dice/deluxe.js`, its own Three.js (0.186) scene with cannon-es physics, both served from the server's packages (`/vendor/three/`, `/vendor/cannon-es.js`). The throw is simulated in one go first (`dice/physics.js`, 120 steps a second, a few ms), then replayed, so no physics runs while drawing. Each die's numbers are turned by one of the solid's symmetries (`relabel` in `dice/shapes.js`, which also holds the d4 to d20 solids) so the server's number ends up on top (a d4 reads at its top corner). Looks: physical materials (clearcoat plastic, metal, see-through ice/glass, iridescent glitter) lit by a studio reflection map, tone mapping, soft shadows on the page, key and rim lights, engraved numbers (bump map), glowing numbers for fire/magic styles, a pulsing glow on a natural 20, natural 1 or max damage, and dice sounds timed to the recorded collisions (Web Audio, the library's sound files). Speed: textures cached per style; shaders compiled when it loads; the first frames measured, and quality steps down (resolution, shadow size, see-through, then shadows off) on a slow device or back up on a fast one, remembered per browser. ~1.7 s a roll.
  - `classic`: [dice-box-threejs](https://github.com/3d-dice/dice-box-threejs) as it comes (Three.js + cannon-es in one self-contained ES module, served at `/vendor/dice/dice-box.js`, sounds under `/vendor/dice/sounds/`; physics, shadows; ~3.5 s a roll: the library only counts dice as stopped after ~3.5 s of simulated time, whatever the hardware).
  If the chosen roller can't start (no WebGL, or its library fails to load), the other is tried, then the result just appears; the tray says so. The dice libraries (dice-box, Three.js, cannon-es) are sent compressed (brotli or gzip, made once per file, with an ETag so a repeat visit gets a 304): ~2.4 MB becomes ~0.4 MB. The chosen roller starts loading when the tray opens or the roller is changed. The effects canvas draws each particle from a picture made once per shape and colour (~17 ms a frame; blurring each particle as drawn took ~370 ms).
- **Dice styles and effects** (owner's request, 2026-10-07): 24 styles in the tray (`STYLES` in `dice.js`: "Match the page" plus colour/texture sets, most adapted from the library's own; textures served from `/vendor/dice/textures/` and fetched only for the style in use). With Classic they use the library's plain or matte materials (its metal and glass need a reflection map the library switches off, so they'd render nearly black); Deluxe gives each style its own physical material. `dice/effects.js` draws on a canvas over the page: each style's trail behind every moving die (positions projected from the 3D scene each frame), a natural 20 (golden flash, rings, stars and confetti at the die, a banner, a shine on the result), a natural 1 (red flash closing in, smoke, a banner, the page shakes) and maxed damage (2+ dice, no d20, all on their highest face). Chimes are synthesised with Web Audio. "Try it" and picking a style throw a preview (rolled in the page, shown only, never recorded). Effects can be switched off; they're always off with reduced motion.
- **Page** (`web/public/dice/dice.js`): the tray (dice buttons and a notation box, Normal / Advantage / Disadvantage for the next d20, dice style, roller, animated / effects / sound switches kept per browser, this session's rolls), the result card (total, every die with dropped ones struck through, natural 20 / natural 1, and a damage roll offered after an attack: doubled dice on a natural 20). On the sheet (owner's request, 2026-10-09), clicking a save, skill, ability name, initiative or spell attack rolls a d20 plus that value (the names that roll are underlined with dots); each attack and spell has a roll to hit (then its damage offered, doubled dice on a natural 20) and a damage roll, with the sheet's modifiers (§6.4 "Attacks and spells that roll"); death saves have one. Shift-click: advantage; Alt-click: disadvantage; or, with no keyboard (phones), the sheet bar's **Next d20** buttons (Normal / Adv. / Disadv., the same setting as the tray's, back to Normal after one d20 roll). Every one of these is an ordinary roll: the server rolls it, the chosen roller shows it, and it's shared like any other ("Sharing" below). **Initiative** from the sheet (`initiative: true` on the roll) also goes into every fight on a map the player can see where one of their player character tokens hasn't rolled yet (§6.6 "Initiative"); the result card says which map. A player's **Roll** button in the map's turn order is the same roll (d20 + the initiative on their sheet, worked out by the server).
- **Sharing** (owner's request, 2026-10-08): every roll goes into the campaign's roll log (`rolls` table, `label` saying what it was) and out live on the campaign's live stream (`GET /campaigns/:cid/live`) to whoever may see it. "Who sees my rolls" in the tray, kept per browser: Everyone (default), Only the DM, or Only me; for the DM, Everyone or Only me (secret). The tray's "Table rolls" list has the last 50 rolls you may see (yours and others', with the character's name, and a lock on private ones); someone else's roll also shows a short note at the top right. The server enforces who sees what. Rolls aren't archived and the archivist doesn't get them (owner's call if wanted).
