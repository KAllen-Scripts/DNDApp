# The DM's wiki (SPEC §6.11)

Owner (2026-10-18): "I want to start work on the DM being able to build a wiki. Our big selling point here is the AI, but of course manual changes and additions should also be possible." Research and options: `/mnt/project-files/research/wiki/campaign-wiki-research.md` (project files). Decisions (2026-10-20): the DM only at first, sharing "at a granular level"; separate from the archivist's records, but Ask reads it; a section the DM edits stays the DM's; the AI's page changes wait for approval; players can't write; the AI suggests reveals and the DM clicks; "If the part of the wiki is available to the players, then their ask should use it."

- **Pages** (`src/wiki/store.js`, `routes/wiki.js`, `web/public/wiki/wiki.js`): title, other names (aliases), kind (free text with suggestions: npc, place, faction…), inside another page (no loops; a deleted page's children move up), tags, and sections (heading, text, `by`: dm or ai, available to). Names are unique across titles and other names; renaming keeps the old title as another name so links keep working. `[[Page]]` and `[[Page|text]]` link pages; a link to a missing page offers to make it; a page lists what mentions it.
- **Available to** (`shared/src/wiki.js`): a page and each section are "only the DM" (a new page's default), "everyone" (a section's "same as the page") or chosen players. Inside a section's text, `:::for Thorin, Mira` … `:::` and `:::dm` … `:::` mark a block (by character or account name; an unknown name reaches no one and the page warns). What a player gets is the narrowest of page, section and block.
- **Saving** needs the version the editor opened (else 409: someone else saved first). Every save is archived whole (`wiki/<id>/changes.jsonl`), so History lists every version and any can be brought back as a new one. Delete takes the page off (the archive keeps it).
- **View as** a player shows exactly what Ask can tell them from the page.
- **Search index:** each part is indexed as a `wiki` doc with `visible_to` its audience; other names as a DM-only doc. Re-indexed on start-up restore and on a rebuild.
- **API** (DM only): `GET/POST /campaigns/:cid/wiki`, `GET/PUT/DELETE /campaigns/:cid/wiki/:pid` (`?as=<user id>` for View as), `GET …/history`, `GET …/history/:version`, `POST …/restore`.
- **The AI's changes** (owner, 2026-10-21; `src/wiki/ai.js`, the "The AI's changes" view of the Wiki tab). After every archivist run (session, correction, sheets and handouts; `WIKI_AUTO=off` turns this off) a `wiki` job reads what the archivist changed since last time (`kb_journal`, 25 records per AI call, task `wiki`: `MODEL_WIKI`, `EFFORT_WIKI` medium) with every page's title, the pages those records are about in full (named in them, or found by searching the wiki), what's waiting and what the DM rejected, and proposes: **new pages** (one per person, place, faction, item, quest, and one per session), **changed sections**, **new sections** and **reveals**. The DM can also press **Read the records now** (for a campaign that had sessions before the wiki).
  - **Who it's available to** is worked out by the server: a proposed section goes to the players who know every record it cites (none valid: only the DM); a new page is as wide as its widest section; a change never widens a section (it can narrow it); a **reveal** offers a section to players who can't see it yet and who know the records cited, and approving it widens the section (and the page, keeping its other sections as they were).
  - **Approving:** Approve, Edit then approve (the edited section is then the DM's), or Reject; Approve all. A section the AI wrote says so on the page and stays `by: "ai"` until the DM changes it (in the editor or by editing a proposal), then it's the DM's for good; for a DM section the AI only makes a "suggestion" when the records disagree. A newer proposal for the same thing replaces one still waiting; a rejected one isn't made again (and the AI is shown it).
  - **Archive and rebuild:** proposals are archived whole after each change (`wiki/proposals.jsonl`) and restored; how far the AI has read is derived, and a rebuild moves it to the end, so replaying every session proposes nothing.
  - API (DM only): `GET …/wiki/proposals`, `POST …/wiki/proposals/run`, `POST …/wiki/proposals/:id/approve` (`{ title?, kind?, page_available?, sections?: [{ heading?, text?, available? }], players? }`), `POST …/wiki/proposals/:id/reject`, `POST …/wiki/proposals/approve-all`. `GET …/wiki` also gives `proposals` (how many wait) and `ai_run` (the last wiki job).
- **Visual** (owner, 2026-10-21; ideas from World Anvil, Kanka, LegendKeeper and Obsidian Portal):
  - **Pictures** (`src/wiki/pictures.js`): the wiki keeps its own copy of every picture it uses: uploaded (chosen, pasted or dropped into the editor) or copied from the DM's creatures, items, merchants, maps (made smaller), handouts or a player's token, the DM choosing. Archived as they came (`wiki/pictures/<id>.<ext>`, a line each in `wiki/pictures/pictures.jsonl`, read from the archive, so nothing to restore). Sent smaller as WebP (480 px for cards and galleries, 1800 px on the page).
  - **A page** has a main picture (in its at-a-glance box), a banner (with where the strip is cut), a gallery, and **facts** (label and value, `[[links]]` allowed: Race, Region, Leader…); each picture and fact says who it's available to ("as the page", only the DM, or chosen players), narrowed by the page. In the text, `![caption](picture:<id>)` puts a picture where its line is; `|right`, `|left`, `|small` or `|wide` after the caption places it. A page can only use the wiki's pictures.
  - **Privacy:** a picture goes only where what it's in goes: a player's copy of a page (View as, Ask) has only the pictures and facts available to them, and a picture in a `:::dm` block or a section they can't see is gone with its text. `GET …/wiki/pictures/:id` is the one wiki route a player may call (for when players read the wiki): the DM gets any picture; a player gets 404, as for one that doesn't exist, unless it's on a part of a page available to them. Ask reads facts as an "At a glance" section and pictures as `[picture: caption]`, never their ids.
  - **Look:** kinds fall into families (people, places, factions, items, creatures, quests, sessions, lore, other; `KIND_FAMILIES` in `shared/src/wiki.js`), each with a colour mixed into the theme and an icon. The front page is picture cards (or the kind's icon) with chips for kinds and tags; a page has a banner with its kind and title (and where it is), the at-a-glance box beside the text (below it on narrow screens), a drop cap, captions, a gallery, a picture shown big on a click, and a preview card (picture, kind, opening words) over a link. On a phone the cards are the list.
  - **The AI:** a new page it proposes is offered the picture of the DM's creature, item, merchant, map or handout with the same name (matched by name on the server, no AI call); the DM keeps or unticks it before approving (`use_picture: false`). A change to a section keeps the pictures the DM put in it.
  - API (DM only unless said): `GET …/wiki/pictures` (`{ pictures, sources }`), `POST …/wiki/pictures` (`{ filename, data }` or `{ from: { kind, id } }`), `GET …/wiki/pictures/:id?size=thumb|full` (players too, filtered as above), `GET …/wiki/picture-sources/:kind/:id` (a small copy for the chooser). Pages take `picture`, `banner`, `gallery` and `facts`; the page list gives each page's `picture` and `excerpt` for the cards and previews.
- **The wiki's space** (owner, 2026-10-27; the build plan is `docs/notes/wiki-space.md`, built stage by stage). The Wiki button leaves the table for a space of its own on the same page (`web/public/wiki/space.js`; `<html data-space="table|wiki">`), so the map's live stream, dice and Ask keep running. The top bar gives way to the wiki's **rail** (a picture button per view: Home, New page, Waiting with its count, and The table to go back to the tab the DM came from) and **bar** (the **trail** of the last 8 pages opened as picture chips, and the in-game time). The address says where the DM is (`?space=wiki#/home`, `#/page/<id>`, `#/page/<id>/edit`, `#/page/<id>/history`, `#/new`, `#/waiting`), so Back, Forward and reload land there; a player sent to it stays at the table. The wiki can be popped out like the other tabs (`?view=wiki`). On a phone the rail is a row of pictures along the top. More views (the Atlas, the Web, the Timeline…) join the rail as they're built.
  - **The Home** (`#/home`, `web/public/wiki/views/home.js`): a hub above every page's card: the campaign's picture (the last session page's, else the picture of the place with most pages inside it, else the families' colours), Last time (the page of the last session played: one named "Session N", else the newest session page), the Wheel small (a slice opens the Wheel there), a tile for each view to come, the party, what waits for the DM, and the pages changed most recently. A front picture the DM picks is left for later: campaign settings reach the players, and a picture id there would be one more thing to filter.
  - **The Wheel** (`#/wheel`, `#/wheel/<family>`, `web/public/wiki/views/wheel.js`): the nine kind families as slices of a colour wheel round the party's token pictures, each with its count (empty ones faded, so the wheel keeps its shape). Picking one pulls its slice out and shows its kinds as chips with counts and its pages as cards.
  - **A page's compass** (`web/public/wiki/compass.js`, `GET …/wiki/:pid/compass`): a strip of picture chips under the banner: its family (to the Wheel), Inside (the chain of pages it's inside, the top first), Holds (the pages inside it), Links to and Linked from (`[[links]]` in its text and facts, both ways). Worked out from what the viewer may see, so a link inside a part they can't see leads nowhere. Its mini-map came with the Atlas; later stages add a ring of people and a ribbon of moments.
  - **Places and tokens link to pages** (stage 2a; maps own the geography, the wiki the words, and nothing is copied between them): a place or token on a map can have `page: { id, title }` (places PATCH, tokens POST and PATCH take `page: { id } | null`; the title is kept so the map can name the page without reading the wiki). `GET …/wiki/:pid` gives `on_maps`, every place and token linked to the page. Renaming a page renames its links, and a place whose name was the old title (`src/wiki/maps.js`, one map change per map, reason `wiki rename`); deleting a page drops its links. Players never get a `page` on a map yet (stage 6 decides what they see). The DM can take part of a map as a picture: `GET …/maps/:mid/image?crop=x,y,w,h&size=` (a small webp), and `POST …/wiki/pictures { from: { kind: 'map', id, crop } }` keeps it in the wiki with its crop.
  - **From either side** (stage 2b): a map place's and token's dialog has a **Wiki page** row (pick a page by title, Open it in the drawer, Unlink, or **Make a page from this**: `POST …/wiki/from-map { map_id, kind, id }` names the page after it, gives it a kind from the map's kind or the token's, a picture cut from the map for a place, puts it inside the page of the place that opens this map, keeps it the DM's, and links it). A token with a page has a Wiki button. A page's **On the map** box (`web/public/wiki/on-map.js`) shows each link as a crop of its map with a pin, and opens the map there on the Map tab. **Revealing in pairs**, never tied together: revealing a linked place asks whether to share the page with the same players too, and a page that's shared but whose place is hidden offers **Also reveal it on the map**. **Send as a handout** (`web/public/wiki/handout.js`, `POST …/wiki/:pid/handout { part, to, share }`): a section, a picture or the at-a-glance box goes to every player or the ones ticked, without any `:::dm` or `:::for` block (links become plain names; the glance box sends only the facts for everyone); `share` also makes that part available to them in the wiki, and the DM is told when the page itself still isn't.
  - **The Atlas** (stage 2c, `#/atlas/<map id>`, `web/public/wiki/views/atlas.js`): the table's own map moved into the wiki's space (not a copy: it keeps its live stream), with the play tools hidden (`<html data-browse>`) and put back at the table on leaving; without a map id it opens the map at the top of the world. **Wiki layers** (`web/public/map/wiki-layers.js`, also from the Map tab's **Wiki** button, remembered in the browser; the DM's only): place pins with the page's picture or icon, faction lands filled with the faction's colour, people at their token or at the place their "Where they are" names, and quests at their "Where" or with whoever their "Given by" names. A pin's card has the picture, kind, opening words, counts (inside, people, quests) and Open page (the drawer at the table) and Open its map. **View as** a player filters the layers (only the places revealed to them and the pages they may see; their fog isn't recreated, and it says so). Server: `GET …/wiki/atlas?map=&as=` (`atlasLayers` in `src/wiki/maps.js`). **Put it on a map** (a page's On the map box): choose a map, then click or drag in the Atlas; the place dialog opens named after the page and linked to it. **Zoom to go in** (stage 2d, the Atlas only): zooming far into a place that opens a map goes into that map, and zooming out past the whole map goes up to the one it's inside, zoomed on its place, each with a short zoom; the address follows. The compass's **mini-map** shows where the page is on its first map (opening the Atlas there), and the Home's Atlas tile shows the top map. Events on the map (after the Timeline) and rumoured places (after truth and rumour) join the layers later.
  - **History shows what changed**: picking a version shows, above it, what changed from the version before (title, sections added, changed or removed, and facts), with the words added and taken out marked (`web/public/wiki/diff.js`, a word diff written for this).
  - **The drawer** (`web/public/wiki/drawer.js`): a page at the side over either space (a sheet on a phone), with its compass, Open in the wiki and Edit; a link inside opens its page there; Escape closes it.
  - **Find** (Ctrl K or Cmd K, the DM's, either space; also Find on the rail; `web/public/wiki/find.js`, `GET …/wiki/find`): picture cards of the pages whose title or other name matches, then up to six whose text matches by words or meaning (the search index's wiki parts the viewer may see), marked "found in its text". In the wiki a card opens the page; at the table, the drawer.
  - **Names as links** (`web/public/wiki/names.js`): page titles and other names in Ask's answers (any case, whole names, never in code) become links that open the drawer. The names come from the DM's page list, so a player's answers are left alone. Map place and token cards get the same in stage 2.
  - **Counts for a viewer** (`src/wiki/views.js`, `GET …/wiki/wheel` and `…/wiki/home`, `?as=` a player): worked out from the pages that viewer may see, so a player's version never counts, names or links a hidden page (a hidden parent is left out). The server keeps each campaign's pages in memory between writes (`src/wiki/store.js`), since the views read them often.
- **Truth and rumour** (stage 3a; owner, 2026-10-27): every section, fact and picture caption has a **canon** (`CANONS` in `shared/src/wiki.js`): `true` (the default), `rumour` (heard, not confirmed), `false` (the players believe it, with the DM's `truth` beside it), `planned` (the DM's prep) or `told` (a player's account, with its `owner`). The server gives a player a falsehood as a rumour and never its truth, and never a planned part, whatever it's available to (`filtered()` in `src/wiki/store.js`; the search index's player docs carry the same marker, and the truth is a DM-only doc). Ask gets a marker before each part (`[rumour]`, `[Thorin's account]`, and for the DM `[false: the players believe this. Truth: …]` and `[planned: …]`), and its instructions say to give rumours as rumours, never to confirm or deny them to a player, and to tell the DM what's true (`PIPELINE_VERSION` 29). On the page (`web/public/wiki/canon.js`, the CSS in one place): rumour dotted with a Rumour badge, false struck red with the truth beside it, planned faint, an account in quote marks; the editor has a picker on each part with a Truth box for a falsehood and whose account it is. The DM's **Truth** button in the bar switches between "what they believe" (everything, marked) and "what's true" (rumours and what's only believed hidden, the truth shown); remembered in the browser. The AI's proposals don't set canon yet, and approving one keeps the canon the DM set.
- **How we know it** (stage 3b): sections and facts keep `sources`, transcript citations (`S12`, `S12 01:02:03`, `S12 01:02:03-01:04:10`; others are dropped, at most 20; `normalizeSources` in `shared/src/citations.js`). Approving the AI's change copies the citations of the records it was written from (their sources, then citations in their text), since record ids change as the archivist works; an updated section keeps its old ones too. Each part shows **From session 12, 1:02:03** chips that open the transcript lines (`GET …/wiki/evidence?s=…&as=`, the same lines Ask's evidence shows, and for a player only sessions they were at; `web/public/wiki/sources.js`). The editor lists a part's sources, each with ✕.
- **Relationships are facts** (stage 3c): a fact whose value links a page ("Rival: [[Lord Vex]]") is a relationship; its label says what it is and its `tone` (`friendly`, `neutral`, `hostile`) how it feels, kept only when the DM picks one in the editor (the picker shows on facts that link a page) and otherwise guessed from the label (`guessTone`, `factTone`, `factLinks` in `shared/src/wiki.js`). On the page such a fact has its tone's colour, the same as the Web's lines will. A page holds up to 60 facts.
- **The Web** (stage 3d, `#/web`, `#/web/<page id>`; `web/public/wiki/views/web.js`, server `src/wiki/web.js`, `GET …/wiki/web?around=&steps=&families=&mentions=1&preset=factions|family&truth=1&as=`): who is tied to whom, worked out on the server from the pages the viewer may see. Lines come from relationship facts (labelled, coloured by tone, styled by canon; your notes dashed, the AI's from play solid), from a page being inside another (a faction or place holding two or more pages in view is drawn as a bubble round them instead) and, on request, from links in the text; two lines between a pair are merged. Round a page: 1 to 3 steps out, the nearest 80; the whole web: the 400 most tied. The layout is `d3-force` from positions derived from the page ids, a fixed 300 steps, so the same pages give the same picture; **Family** lays out generations in rows from parent, child, married and sibling labels; **Factions** keeps faction pages and their ties. A node's card has Open page, Centre here and **Add a link…** (a new fact saved with the page's version). View as a player, and the Truth button (rumours and falsehoods left out), follow. On a phone, a page's web is a ring round it and a tap re-centres. The compass gains **Ties** (the six nearest, and a Web button) and the Home's Web tile is live. A player's web never has a hidden page, or a line from a part they can't see (a DM-only or planned fact, a `:::dm` link); for the DM each line says who it's available to.
- **The AI proposes ties** (stage 3e): besides pages, sections and reveals, the AI's changes include `set_fact`, a relationship as a fact on an existing page (label from that page's side, the other page linked, a tone or none, `true` or `rumour` from the records' wording; it can never make a part planned or an account), and a new page can carry its ties. Who a tie is available to comes from the records' `known_by`, never wider than the fact it replaces. The card under Waiting shows what the page says now beside it; Edit changes its words, tone and who it's for. Approving copies the records' citations; a fact the DM had marked (a rumour, false, planned, an account) keeps that mark; a tie saved unchanged stays the AI's (`by: 'ai'`, drawn solid on the Web). A rejected tie isn't proposed again. The AI now sees each page's facts. `PIPELINE_VERSION` 30.
- **The calendar** (stage 4a; `shared/src/calendar.js`, `calendar` in the campaign's settings): `{ preset, name, months: [{ name, days, leap?, festival? }], leap: { every, except, unless } | null, weekdays, weekday, start: { year, month, day }, era, moons: [{ name, days, full }], seasons: [{ name, month, day }], holidays: [{ name, month, day }] } | null`. Null (the default, and every campaign from before) is "Day 14, 18:30" as always. Presets: Harptos (festival days named on their own, Shieldmeet every fourth year, Selûne full every 30.4375 days, seasons), Gregorian-like (Gregorian leap years, weekdays, a 29.53-day moon), Exandria (written from memory) and the DM's own, all editable in Campaign settings → Calendar. The clock stays minutes since day 1 began; `start` names day 1. `clockText(minutes, calendar)`: "3 Mirtul 1492 DR, 18:30 · Full moon" (only full and new moons are mentioned; with more moons, by name). Every clock text uses it: the map's clock and its dialog, travel, rests' notes, the wiki bar and the map events the archivist reads (`PIPELINE_VERSION` 31).
- **When, and events** (stage 4b; `shared/src/wiki-time.js`): a page has `when` (`{ text, from, to } | null`) and `events` (`[{ id, when, text, available, canon, truth?, owner?, sources?, by? }]`, up to 100). Times are minutes of the campaign's clock (0 = the start of Day 1; earlier is negative); `to` is where it ends (the same as `from` for a moment); both are null when the text isn't a time the parser knows, and the text is kept. The DM (and later the AI) writes only the text; the server reads it with the campaign's calendar and clock when it's saved (`parseWhen`), and keeps the time while the text stays the same, so "300 years ago" doesn't drift as the clock moves. Understood: "Day 12", "Day 12, 21:00", "Day 12 night" (dawn, morning, noon, afternoon, dusk, evening, night, midnight), calendar dates with or without the year, era, weekday or a time, whole months and years, festival days, ranges, "N years/months/tendays/weeks/days/hours ago" (from now), "N … before" (before Day 1), "today". `whenText` writes a time back by the calendar ("3 Mirtul 1492 DR dusk", "Mirtul 1492 DR", "1192 DR"; with no calendar before Day 1, "300 years before Day 1"). A player never gets a planned event or one not available to them, and gets a falsehood as a rumour; the search index and Ask's reading of a page list the events dated. The page shows its time under the title and its events in order (undated last); the editor shows what each time reads as while it's typed.
- **The Timeline** (stage 4c; `#/timeline`, `#/timeline/<page id>`; `web/public/wiki/views/timeline.js`, server `src/wiki/timeline.js`, `GET …/wiki/timeline?as=&truth=1`): pages with a time and every event, worked out for the viewer from the pages they may see (a player never gets a hidden page, a planned event, one not available to them, or a place not revealed to them). Lanes: the DM's own (`wiki: { lanes: [page ids] }` in the campaign's settings, up to 12; a lane holds its page, the pages inside it and the pages linking to it), then **History** (anything that began before Day 1) and **The campaign**; an item goes in the first lane that claims it, and an empty History is left out. Pages are picture cards, events slim bars styled by truth (a rumour dotted, false or planned faint) and by who wrote them (your notes striped). Dragged sideways, zoomed from centuries to hours; the scale says the calendar's years, months and days (or Day N) and marks full moons; a line marks now. Undated items and session pages with no time sit in trays. A card has Open page and Show on the map (the Atlas at the place, its pin pulsing); the Atlas card's On the timeline goes back. On a phone it's a vertical ribbon. The compass's When mini-ribbon and the Home's Timeline tile open it. At most 600 items.
- **The AI dates things** (stage 4d): `set_when` (a page's time; a session page's is the in-game span it covered) and `add_event` (a dated line on the page it's most about, linking the others, with canon, sources and who it's for from `known_by`), in the campaign's calendar; a new page can carry both. The DM approves each on Waiting (Time and Event cards, editable). **Journeys** on the maps are bars on The campaign lane, worked out from the maps' archived changes (never stored twice; for a player only maps shown to them and tokens not hidden).
- **Templates** (stage 5a; `#/templates`): a new page starts from its kind's template (the DM's own first, then the app's starters: Person, which leads with **At the table**, Place, Faction, Item, Creature, Quest, Session, Lore, Clue, Arc), with writing prompts in its empty sections and facts; sections left unwritten aren't kept. The DM's templates are pages with `template: true`, always the DM's and kept out of the wiki (its list, Ask, the views, the AI, maps and players). Any page can be saved as a template.
- **The AI as a writing partner** (stage 5b; on the DM's buttons, one AI call each, task `wiki_draft`; results wait under Waiting like the AI's other changes): **Draft from the records** (fill a page, or a new one with its template's prompts, from what the records say, with audiences from `known_by`), **Expand** a section (from the records; never wider), **Tidy** a section (the wording only; who it's available to stays), **Make up…** (in Find and on the Home: an NPC, a tavern, a shop, a rumour, a name, consistent with the wiki, which it reads first; a page only the DM sees, marked planned). **Link it**: names in a page's text that match another page are underlined, and one press makes the link (matched on the server, no AI).
- **Players read their own wiki** (stage 6a; the owner's plan assumed yes): the Wiki tab is everyone's. A player's wiki is the DM's filtered to them on the server (`wiki.listFor(cid, viewer)`: each page through `filtered()`, a parent they can't see dropped, and `[[links]]` to pages they can't see turned into the words shown, so a hidden title never leaks); every read route (`GET …/wiki`, a page, the Home, the Wheel, Find, the Web, the Timeline, the Atlas, the compass, the evidence) treats a player as `?as=` themselves, a hidden page is a 404, and the Atlas only lists maps shown to them. The page leaves out the DM's tools: New page, Templates, Waiting, the truth switch, Edit, History, View as, Save as a template, Delete, Make up…, Link it, and who a part is available to; the DM's addresses open the Home instead. Ask's names come from `listFor` too.
- **Character pages** (stage 6b): a page can be a player's character's (`player`, one page per player, only a player of the campaign). Waiting offers one for each character with a name and no page (`characters` in `GET …/wiki/proposals`; `POST …/wiki/characters {player}` makes it: the character's name, kind `character`, for everyone, with a copy of their token picture). The page shows **From the sheet** (`from_sheet` with the page: race, class and level; age, height, eyes and the rest with the appearance text; backstory; allies; `sheetParts` in `shared/src/wiki.js`), worked out from the active sheet on every read and never copied into the wiki. The sheet is private, so the DM and the player see every part and the other players only those the player shares (`sheet_share`, saved with `PUT …/wiki/:pid/sheet-share`, by the player or the DM). The party in the middle of the Wheel and on the Home opens their pages.
- **Players' lore** (stage 6c; owner, 2026-10-27: players may contribute lore about their own characters): on their character's page a player has **Your story** with **Add to your story** (`POST …/wiki/lore`, players only): a section on their character's page, a page of its own (inside their character's), a relationship (a fact) or an event, each for only the DM (the default), the DM and chosen players, or everyone. It becomes a proposal (`from: 'player'`, `by_player`) under Waiting, marked as from them, where the DM approves it (edited or not) as **their account** (`told`, the default), **true**, or **false** with the truth beside it, or rejects it; `GET …/wiki/lore` shows the player theirs and what became of each. Approved parts are `by: 'player'` with `owner`; only their player can change them (`PUT …/wiki/:pid/lore`, at once, and one the DM confirmed as true goes back to being their account); the DM changing one makes it the DM's. The AI never rewrites them (no update_section, Expand or Tidy on them; its instructions say so; a tie of theirs is never replaced). Ask already gives them as "Thorin's account". `PIPELINE_VERSION` 34. The page offers Change on a player's own sections; their facts and events change through the API for now.
