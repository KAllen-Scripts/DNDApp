# Maps (SPEC §6.6)

Combat on maps is in [map-combat.md](map-combat.md), the world and travel in [world.md](world.md), the map creator in [map-creator.md](map-creator.md), the DM's creatures in [creatures-items.md](creatures-items.md).

Owner's requirements (2026-10-07): no premade maps. The DM imports their own map (a battle map, a town, a region, anything); the map itself is just the terrain. Since 2026-10-11 the DM can also **make a map from scratch** in the app (owner: "I also want to be able to create maps from scratch. Meaning we want assets! And a fully fleshed out map creator. Let's start with the basics and we will build it up from there"); a made map is an ordinary map from then on (see Making a map below). The AI reads it when the DM asks (Map settings → Read with the AI; since 2026-10-16 not on import, owner: "That shouldn't be automatic") and turns it into something interactive. The DM then adds tokens by hand for characters, enemies and NPCs. **Players move their own token; the DM can move any.** Everyone sees moves live.

- **Import** (DM, Map tab): a PNG, JPEG or WebP image (up to the upload limit). The image is archived exactly as uploaded (`maps/<id>/image.<ext>`); the page shows that file. The map starts hidden from players.
- **Showing a map to the players** (owner, 2026-10-20, after his player's page stayed on the old map while the DM was on a new one): a map is **shown** (Map settings → Players can see this map) or not, and players can look at any shown map from their map list. The DM's **Show players this map** button, beside the map list (out in full screen too, in ⋯ on a phone), shows the map on screen if it was hidden and makes it **the players' map** (`POST .../bring` sets `brought_at`; `playersMap` in `shared/src/map.js`: the shown map brought to last). Every player's page goes there at once (the live `map` update carries the new `brought_at`) and says "Your DM brought everyone here" in its status line. On the players' map the button reads **Players are here**; the DM's map list marks it "(players here)", the players' "(party here)". Players can still look at another shown map; only the next bring moves them again. A player's page opens on the players' map (else the one they last had open, else the newest); when the map on screen is hidden it goes to the players' map, and back to the hidden one if it's shown again before they pick another. When the players would see only dark fog there (fog on, nothing revealed, and line of sight off or no player character on the map), the DM's status line says so and what to do. The archivist hears "The DM brought the players to the map …" (Map events).
- **A page of a PDF** (adventures and map packs often come as PDFs): the DM picks the page number. The server draws that page with pdf.js on a native canvas (`@napi-rs/canvas`), 3000 px on the long side (at most 6× the page's size), on white, and uses the PNG as the map's image. The PDF is archived next to it (`maps/<id>/source.pdf`) and the map records which page (`source`).
- **Reading (AI, in the background after import, or "Read again"):** the server makes a smaller JPEG copy (2000 px on the long side) and asks the AI (`maps` task) what kind of map it is, a name, a short description of the terrain for the DM, whether it has a grid (and roughly how many squares across and down), and its scale (from a scale bar or labels, e.g. "1 square = 5 ft" or "the map is 30 miles across"). Where the AI sees a grid, the server measures it exactly from the full image's pixels (`detectGrid`: edge strength per column and row, the repeat distance near the AI's estimate, then the offset), because the AI's counts are only approximate. A grid with no scale gets 5 ft per square. The DM can correct the grid (square size and offset, shown on the map while editing) and the scale. The map is usable while it's being read; a read interrupted by a restart is marked failed and can be run again.
- **Tokens:** the DM adds them with a kind (player character, NPC, enemy, companion or summon), a name, a size (Tiny to Gargantuan: ½, 1, 2, 3, 4 squares) and a colour. A player character token is tied to a player in the campaign (named after their character); that player can move it. It shows the token picture that player uploaded (§6.4), or initials until they do. The DM can give an NPC or enemy token a picture (owner's request, 2026-10-08): "Picture" on the selected token; if other tokens on the map have the same name (a pack of goblins), the DM is asked whether they all get it. The upload is archived as it came (`maps/<id>/tokens/<file>`); the token keeps `art: { file, type }`, which never leaves the server (the page gets `picture`, its key) and is served cut to a square around what stands out, only to people who can see that token. "No picture" goes back to initials. On a grid, tokens snap to squares; on a map without one they're placed freely, sized relative to the map. While dragging, the page shows the distance moved (5e counting on a grid: every square, diagonals included, is one square; straight-line distance otherwise).
- **Companions and summons** (owner's request, 2026-10-14): a token of kind `companion` belongs to a player (`user_id`), like their character's: a familiar, an animal companion, a steed, a conjured creature. The player adds one with **Companion** on the map toolbar (players only; the DM uses Add token: pick the player under "Who is it?" and tick "A companion or summon of theirs"): a name, size, maximum hit points and darkvision, and by default its stat block looked up with the AI as for the DM's tokens, except that a player's lookup never copies the DM's own creatures (they're the DM's). It appears where the player is looking if they can see that spot, else next to their character (a player can't place one under the fog). Up to 12 per player per map (`MAX_COMPANIONS`). The player moves it (walls stop it, as their character), changes its hit points and conditions, light and darkvision, and its name, size, colour, speed and stat block; **Dismiss** takes it off the map. The DM can do anything to it. Everyone who sees it sees its hit points in full, like a party member's; its stat block goes only to its player (and the DM). Its player sees what it sees (it counts as one of their tokens for line of sight, so a familiar can scout), opens doors next to it and takes it through links. **Roll** on the selected companion opens its rolls, read from its stat block (`statBlockRolls`): each attack's roll to hit (with its damage offered after) and damage, and the six ability checks; they go through the dice like any roll, labelled with its name. In a fight it rolls initiative from its stat block like an NPC; its player can type its roll and end its turn. The archivist hears about it as "Name (a companion or summon)". Saved to the DM's creatures it becomes an NPC.
- **Companions kept on the sheet** (2026-10-21): a companion token can be tied to an entry in its player's Companions box (§6.4): then its name, stat block and hit points are the sheet's, a hit point change goes on the sheet, it's on one map at a time, and it goes when the sheet's entry is dismissed; Dismiss on the map takes it off the map but leaves it on the sheet.
- **Fog of war** (DM, **Fog & sight**, a play tool: fog, Unseen parts, Reveal/Cover, Line of sight, Remember explored, Darkness, Show walls): when it's on, players see only the parts the DM has revealed (turning it on starts the DM in Reveal mode, since everything begins covered). The DM drags rectangles to reveal or cover again (snapped to squares on a grid), can undo, reveal all or cover all. Fog is a list of shapes applied in order (`fog.shapes`, up to 1000). Players get the picture as it is and the DM's rectangles, and their page draws the fog over it; tokens under the fog are left out for them by the server (except their own). (Until 2026-10-14 the server blacked out each player's copy of the picture; redrawing it on every move took seconds on big maps, and the owner decided that what a determined player could dig out of their browser doesn't matter.) The DM sees the fog as a shade over the map.
- **Walls and line of sight** (DM, drawn in **Edit map**; Line of sight is ticked in Fog & sight): walls are straight lines in image pixels (`walls`: `{ id, x1, y1, x2, y2, door, open, locked, secret?, revealed?, source, group? }`, up to 2000; `source` is `dm`, `ai`, `file` or `creator` (from a map made in the creator)); a door is a wall that can be open, and locked (players can't open it). An **obstacle** (`kind: 'low'`) blocks movement but not sight: a building drawn as its roof, a cliff edge, a fence. The DM drags to draw a wall or door (ends snap to other walls' ends, then grid corners, so walls join without gaps), draws a **curved wall** with Curve (drag from end to end, move to bend it, click) or a **round wall** with Circle (drag from the centre out), clicks to erase (a curve's pieces share a `group` and are erased together; walls PATCH `curve: {x1, y1, mx, my, x2, y2}` and `circle: {x, y, r}`, stored as pieces of about 10°, a circle as 36), and in Lock mode clicks a door to lock it. Outside the drawing tools, the DM clicks a door to pick it: the bar under the map says whether it's closed, open or locked, with Open/Close, Lock/Unlock and Remove (opening a door unlocks it; locking one closes it). **Secret doors** (2026-10-13): Make secret turns a door into one (`secret: true`, walls PATCH `secret: id`); players see wall there (it isn't in their `doors`) until it's found (`revealed`): the DM presses Reveal (`reveal: id`; Hide again shuts it and makes it wall to players once more), or someone opens it. The DM sees it dashed. Map events tell the archivist when one was found. Doors are drawn as doors, not coloured lines: posts at both sides of the doorway, a thick plank across it with a badge in the middle (a door, or a red padlock when locked, which players see too), and when open the door stands swung from its first end with a dotted arc. A player clicks a door to open or close it, only a door they can see (players get `doors`: the doors they can see, never walls), not locked, within a square and a half of their token (`POST .../doors/:wid/toggle`). **AI walls** (task `walls`: the maps model at `xhigh` effort by default, `MODEL_WALLS` / `EFFORT_WALLS`; in the background, `wall_draft` status) is deliberately thorough, since a map is set up once (owner, 2026-10-08: worth being expensive; 2026-10-10: `max` used about 30% of a session's tokens, so one level down). The AI sees the map with a **ruler** round it (ticks every 50 and numbers every 100 of 0 to 1000 on each side, faint lines across every 100) so it reads positions off it; it answers with straight walls and obstacles as polylines, **curves** as arcs (start, a point halfway, end) and **circles** (centre and a point on the edge), doors, lights and difficult terrain (purpose `map:walls`). Then it **checks** its draft: the draft is drawn over the map (walls red, obstacles green, curves orange, doors blue) and sent back with its data to be corrected (purpose `map:walls-check`). Maps over 1800 px wide or tall are checked in **close-ups** (up to 3 × 3, overlapping a little, each with a ruler in the whole map's positions); each close-up keeps what lies in its own part (by the middle of each piece), and a close-up whose check fails keeps the first draft there (the notes say so). The server then tidies straight walls (`tidyWalls` in `maps/read.js`): nearly level or upright ones (within 4°) are straightened, ends that nearly meet (1% of the long side, or a third of a square) are joined, and with a grid, ends within 0.3 of a square of a grid line are put on it. Curves keep their shape (only their two ends join other walls). So a draft costs 2 to 10 calls at xhigh effort. (Proposed, not merged: PR #33, the owner's request 2026-10-11, adds an effort picker beside AI walls, default the server's setting or low to max, kept per browser, sent as `effort` with the draft.) The draft replaces the AI's earlier one; walls the DM drew stay; AI walls show in another colour and can be cleared. **Progress** (owner, 2026-10-10: clicking AI walls gave no feedback): while it works, `wall_draft` carries `step` (`tracing`, then `checking` with `parts` and `parts_done` as each close-up's check comes back, then `tidying`) and `started_at`, updated live (`onProgress` from `mapReader.walls`). **The tracing so far is drawn on the map as it goes** (owner, 2026-10-16): `onProgress` carries a `preview` (the first draft, with each checked close-up swapped in as it comes back), which replaces the AI's walls, lights, terrain, stairs and raised areas on the map like the finished draft does; when it ends, `finished_at` and, when done, `found` (counts of the walls, obstacles, doors, lights and areas of difficult terrain it kept). Players get none of it. On the page the button reads "AI walls: starting…" at once, then "AI walls: working…" with a spinner, disabled; a message over the top of the map (so it shows in full screen and outside Edit map) says what the AI is doing and how long so far, that it takes a few minutes and the DM can carry on; then "AI walls done: 12 walls, 3 doors and 2 lights in 4 min" with the AI's notes, or the reason it failed with **Try again**. The DM closes the done or failed message; it stays closed in that browser (`dndapp.map.wallNoteSeen.<map>`) until the next draft. **Walls from a file:** a Universal VTT file (`.dd2vtt` from Dungeondraft, `.uvtt`, `.df2vtt`; Dungeon Alchemist and many map packs export one) gives exact walls (`line_of_sight` and `objects_line_of_sight`), doors (`portals`, open if not `closed`) and lights (`range` in squares, half bright, half dim), with no AI (`maps/uvtt.js`). Imported as a map, its picture becomes the map, its grid is used exactly (the AI's reading doesn't replace it) and squares are 5 ft. Added to an existing map (Edit map → Walls from file, `POST .../walls/file`), it's stretched to fit the map's picture and replaces the walls and lights from an earlier file; the DM's and the AI's stay. **Hiding walls:** Fog & sight has a Show walls tick box (per browser): unticked, walls, obstacles and lights aren't drawn outside Edit map, for a clean map while playing; doors still show (they're clicked). With **Line of sight** (`fog.sight`) on as well as the fog, each player also sees what their own player-character tokens have a clear line to: a visibility polygon from the token's centre, cut by walls and closed doors (`sightPolygon` in `shared/src/map.js`). **Unseen parts** (`fog.map`): what players get outside their sight and the DM's reveals: `dark` (blacked out), `grey` (greyed out: dimmed) or `shown` (the map as it is); tokens there are hidden in all three. With **Remember explored** (`fog.memory`, on by default, only meaningful when dark), places a player has seen stay on their map dimmed: a grid of 128 cells along the long side per player per map (`map_explored` table, schema v10, database only: not archived, so a rebuild forgets it; the DM can "Forget explored"). A player sees a token only if it's in their sight or in a part the DM revealed (dim, explored areas don't show tokens). The DM's rectangles still apply outside sight. The server works out what each player sees for deciding which tokens, doors, links, templates and terrain they get, and marks explored places (`maps/sight.js`). The page draws the fog itself (`playerFogMask` in `shared/src/map.js`, the same code): players get the walls that block as plain lines (`walls`: `{ x1, y1, x2, y2, kind }`, closed doors and unfound secret doors among them, as wall), the lights (`lights`: placed ones and those carried by tokens they don't get), the DM's rectangles and `fog.explored` (rectangles of the cells they've seen). While a player drags their own token the fog follows it, from where it would land, unless a wall is in the way. Dropped past a wall, the token goes back at once with the server's message and no move is sent; dropped anywhere else, the token shows where it lands but the fog still sees from where it was until the server takes the move (`state.landing`), so a refused move never shows what's past a wall (owner, 2026-11-09: it flashed the view from the far side). `sightPolygon` buckets walls into a grid of cells and walks each ray through it, so a map with 2000 wall pieces takes milliseconds; lit areas are kept per set of walls and shared by every player. While the map is panned or zoomed the stage is its own layer (`will-change: transform`, cleared once it settles), so the fog isn't redrawn every frame. Players can't move their token through a wall, obstacle or closed door (a straight line from where it was); the DM can put any token anywhere. Each player sees only from their own tokens, not their party's.
- **Height** (owner's request, 2026-10-15, after a brainstorm: research in the Claude project, `research/elevation/`): the DM's **Raised area** tool in Edit map drags out a rectangle at the height in the box next to it (feet above the map's ground, −1000 to 1000; negative for a pit); a click on one gives it the box's height; Erase removes it (`raised`: `{ id, points, height, source }`, up to 300, `PATCH .../raised`). Each is flat on top; where they overlap the highest counts (`groundAt`). The DM sees their outlines and heights in Edit map. Tokens have a **height** (`height` in feet, set in the token's bar as Tall; empty: a player character's sheet Height, read by `parseHeight` and sent as `tall`, else by size: Tiny 2, Small and Medium 6, Large 10, Huge 20, Gargantuan 30) and an **elevation** (`elevation`, Up in the bar: feet above the ground under it, for flying or climbing); players set their own. A token not on the ground shows ↑25 or ↓10. **Sight over heights** (the standard terrain line-of-sight test, as GIS viewsheds and Foundry's height modules do it): with line of sight on, a player sees a token only if, as well as being in their sight, one of their tokens has a line from its eyes (1 ft below the top of its head) to the top of the other's head that clears every raised edge it crosses: at each edge, `eye + (head − eye) × how far along ≥ the edge's higher side`. So someone on a roof is seen from far enough away but not from under its edge, the ground behind a building is hidden, tall and flying creatures see and are seen over lower things, and it's the same both ways. The DM's reveals show tokens whatever the heights. Walls still block whatever the height. Players get the raised areas (`raised`: `{ points, height }`), and their page's fog over heights (`heightShadows`) comes in **two shades** (owner's choice, 2026-10-20: a tall creature up on a roof is seen before a short one): clear where one of their tokens sees the floor, a light shade (`glimpse`, 30%) where none does but one would see a Medium creature standing there, fog where they'd see neither; so a roof seen from below is lightly shaded. It's outlined smoothly like a wall's shadow (owner, 2026-10-20: whole squares looked blocky beside the walls' shadows): points a square apart, then an eighth of a square apart where those differ, traced into outlines (marching squares) whose crossings are found by halving to a hundredth of the step, sent as `{ fill, rings }` and filled even-odd; explored places stay dim. Not yet: edges blocking movement, cover hints, heights in the explored-places memory.
- **Checking and changing heights** (owner, 2026-10-18): in Edit map (or with the Raised area tool on), a click on a raised area picks it: it turns yellow with handles, and the bar under the map shows its height in a box (changing it sets it), a **Bridge** tick box, Remove, and for the AI's guesses "Looks right" (keeps it as it is) and "Next guess (n)". **Check AI heights (n)** in Edit map picks the first guess (top to bottom) and brings it into view; "Looks right, next" goes on to the next. Accept all keeps every guess. The picked area's corners drag (onto half squares with a grid), a dot halfway along each side drags out a new corner, a corner dropped on its neighbour goes (at least three stay), and a drag from inside moves the whole area (`set: {id, height?, points?, bridge?}`; any change makes the AI's guess the DM's).
- **Bridges** (owner, 2026-10-18): a raised area with `bridge: true` (the tick box, the Bridge box beside Raised area for a new one, or the AI's guess: its raised areas have `bridge` for bridges, walkways and galleries; PIPELINE_VERSION 20) is a deck 1 ft thick (`BRIDGE_DECK_FT`) with open space under it. Tokens have `under` (players set their own; the bar's "Under the bridge" box shows on a token in one). Moving sets it (`levelWalker` / `walkLevels` in `shared/src/map.js`, also in `pathCost`): a token walks onto a bridge where it comes to it from no more than 5 ft below the deck (`BRIDGE_STEP_FT`) or by stairs or a ladder, otherwise under it, and stays on (or under) until it leaves it; climbing costs as before. A token under one stands on the ground beneath (`tokenGround`), is drawn faded with a dashed ring and an "under" badge, and the drag readout says "under the bridge". Sight: bridges aren't ground edges; a line of sight is blocked where, over the bridge, its height runs through the deck (`throughDeck`), so people under it and out on the bank see each other, someone on it and someone under it don't, and the fog shows a square if someone standing there on the bridge or under it would be seen. Players get `bridge` on their raised areas. Kept in .dd2vtt exports. Not yet: bridges in the map creator.
- **Stairs and ladders** (owner's request, 2026-10-16): `climbs`: `{ id, kind: stairs | ladder, x1, y1, x2, y2, source }`, a line up each from the bottom to the top, up to 300 (`PATCH .../climbs`: add, remove, clear `ai` or `all`). The DM drags them with **Stairs** and **Ladder** in Edit map (purple lines with a dot at the top, shown in Edit map; Erase removes them). The AI wall draft traces them too (`climbs` in its schema, drawn purple on the checking pass's picture; Clear AI removes them); the map creator's stairs, spiral stairs and ladder pieces become them, unless they lead to another map. **Movement** (`pathCost`): wherever a path goes from one ground height to another, the nearest stairs or ladder within ¾ of a square of the crossing decides: stairs cost nothing more; a ladder, or nothing (a bare wall: `wall`, the readout says "no ladder or stairs" so the DM can ask for Athletics), is climbing, each foot up or down costing a foot extra (5e), in the map's unit. A token with a climbing speed (its stat block's speed, or the Wild Shape form's: `climber` in the view) or flying (Up above 0) climbs for nothing. The drag readout shows "climbs N ft", and in a fight the climbing counts against the turn. Players get the climbs (`{ kind, x1, y1, x2, y2 }`) for their readout. Moves are never refused for climbing.
- **Heights in the map creator** (owner's request, 2026-10-16): designs keep a height in feet for every half square (`heights`: two rows of numbers per row of squares, two per square, −1000 to 1000, 0 the ground; older designs read as all ground, and one saved with a number per square reads as halves). The **Height** tool (H) takes the feet in its panel: a click gives the height to every half square joined to it with the same floor and height and no wall between (as the bucket fills: a whole room, tower or wall-walk, even one between walls half a square apart), a drag to a rectangle on half squares; 0 puts them back on the ground. The heights show (blue up, brown down, with their feet) only while the tool is picked. Copy, cut, paste, turning, stamps, resizing and moving the map carry them with their squares. On saving, `designToMap` gives them (`heightAreas`) as a circle inside each round wall (the Round tool's) whose half squares all share one height, so round towers are round, and rectangles of one height each for the rest, and the map's raised areas from the creator (`source: 'creator'`) are replaced by them; ones the DM drew on the map stay. Saving again keeps the scale the DM set on the map (a new map gets 5 ft squares).
- **AI walls guess heights** (owner, 2026-10-16): the AI wall draft traces buildings drawn as roofs, flat tower tops (round ones as circles), castle wall-walks, cliff tops, platforms and pits as `raised` (an outline or a circle, and its guess at the height in feet: a house about 10, two storeys 20, a castle wall 25, a tower 40), drawn cyan with their heights on the checking pass's picture; obstacles are now only fences, hedges, low walls and deep water's edge. They're stored as `source: 'ai'` (a new draft replaces them, Clear AI removes them) and count in the done message. The DM sees them dashed with "10 ft?"; with Raised area, a click on one accepts it (and puts its height in the box, so a second click after changing the box corrects it), and **Accept AI heights (n)** accepts them all (`PATCH .../raised` `accept: id | all`; setting a height also accepts). Accepted ones are the DM's and stay through later drafts. Guesses count for sight and movement as soon as they arrive. The picture doesn't show heights; the stairs and ladder pieces are how to get up.
- **Hit points and conditions:** a token can have hit points (current and max) and any of the 5e conditions plus concentrating. The DM sets them; a player can set their own token's. **A player character's token uses its sheet's hit points** (owner, 2026-10-10): once the player has saved a sheet, the token shows the sheet's current and maximum (`hp_sheet: true`), and changing its hit points on the map saves the new current hit points to the sheet (`setSheetHp`, reason "hit points changed on the map", by whoever changed it); the maximum is always the sheet's (greyed out in the DM's token dialog). Any sheet save (the player's, a rest, a purchase) sends the maps with that character's token again, and the player's page hears `sheet` on the campaign's live stream and loads the new version. A druid in a Wild Shape form with its own hit points (2014 rules, §6.4) shows the form's instead, and its walking speed; a change on the map goes to the form, and at 0 they change back with the rest of the damage carried over to the sheet. Without a saved sheet the token keeps its own hit points. In the selection bar, typing `-7`, `+5` or `12` changes current hit points. Players see the numbers only for player characters; for NPCs and enemies they get how hurt it looks (unhurt, hurt, bloodied at half or less, down), drawn as a bar.
- **Hidden tokens:** the DM can hide a token from players (an ambush, someone lurking). Players never get it; the DM sees it faded.
- **How stat blocks look:** every stat block the DM sees (token and creature "Stat block" dialogs, the creature Edit dialog) is Markdown rendered through `markdown.js` with the `stat-block` class: the name in the accent colour in small caps, accent rules above and below, accent section headings and labels, the ability scores centred between thin rules.
- **Stat blocks (AI, DM only):** for an enemy or NPC token, the DM can have the AI fill in its 5e stat block. In order (owner's request, 2026-10-09): **the DM's own creatures first**: if a creature of that name on the Creatures tab has a stat block ("Goblin 3" and "a goblin" match "Goblin"; never the creature being filled itself), it is copied as it is, with its hit points and size, and no AI call (`from: 'your creatures (Goblin)'`); **then the group's books**: if a book in `BOOKS_DIR` prints the creature's stat block (`books.findCreature`: a name line followed by a size-and-type line such as "Small humanoid (goblinoid), neutral evil" and, within a few lines, "Armor Class" or the 2024 books' "AC"; matched like spells, despite OCR mix-ups; "Goblin 3" and "a goblin" find the Goblin), the AI copies it from that page (purpose `map:stats-book`; `source: 'book'`, `from: 'Monster Manual, page 166'`). Only when no book has it does the AI write it from its own knowledge (purpose `map:stats`, `source: 'ai'`): name, AC, hit point formula, speed, challenge and the block as Markdown. It also sets the token's hit points (the average) and size, unless the DM already set them. A new enemy is looked up automatically (a checkbox in the add dialog). The DM can look up a different creature instead (e.g. the token is "Boss" but it's a Bugbear). If the AI doesn't know the creature, nothing changes. Players never get stat blocks.
- **NPCs from the campaign's records:** when adding a token, the DM picks who it is from one **Who is it?** list (players, the DM's creatures, then the records; owner's request 2026-10-14, the separate fields overlapped), so the DM can pick someone from the archivist's records (people and creatures first; the archivist names its kinds freely, so that's a guess from the kind). The token is named after them and linked to the record (`record: { id, title }`), and the DM can open what the records say about them from the map. The title is kept with the id because ids change when the knowledge base is rebuilt; the record is found by title then. Players only get the token's name.
- **Turning the map** (owner, 2026-10-14): on a touch screen, twisting two fingers turns the map around them, once they've twisted 15° (a pinch to zoom always twists a little), along with the pinch zoom. It's each screen's own view, like zoom (`state.view.r`), never saved or shared. Tokens, pins and links stay upright. While it's turned, an N button in the top left corner points at north; tapping it turns the map back, keeping the zoom. Letting go within 8° of north straightens it, and Fit (or another map) resets it. The finger that stays still in a pinch is never taken as a tap.
- **Private pins:** anyone who can see a map can drop pins on it with a label and colour, as notes to themselves. Only they ever see their pins, not even the DM. They're kept apart from the map (`map_pins` table, schema v8; `maps/<id>/pins/<user id>.jsonl` in the archive, the whole list on each line), never sent over the live stream, and restored from the archive. Up to 100 per person per map. They stay the same size on screen at any zoom.
- **Measure** (anyone, only on their own screen): with Measure on, dragging on the map draws a line and shows the distance; on a grid both ends sit in the middle of a square and it counts squares the 5e way (`measure`). It can start on a token without moving it. The line stays until the next one or until Measure is switched off. While a token is dragged, its path is drawn too.
- **Darkness, lights and darkvision** (owner's request, 2026-10-08, from the research): with fog and line of sight on, the DM can turn on Darkness (`fog.dark`: night, or underground). Then each player sees only lit places their token has a line to, plus what its darkvision reaches (`darkvision` on the token, in the map's unit). Lights are placed by the DM from presets (candle, torch, lamp, lantern, the Light cantrip, a campfire; `lights`: `{ id, x, y, bright, dim, source }`, up to 200) or carried by a token (`light: { bright, dim }`, which a player can set on their own). The AI's wall draft also suggests the lights it sees on the picture (torches, braziers, fires, magic), which "Clear AI draft" removes with the AI's walls. Players get where the lights are (for their page's fog, since 2026-10-14); `sightOf` gives lit areas as `{ points, clip }`: the lit circle cut to what the token can see, drawn clipped on the page's fog canvas. The fog is a `<canvas>` over the picture (at most 2048 px across), not an SVG `<mask>`: a phone browser silently drew nothing for the mask once it held a token's sight. Lights nowhere near what a token sees are left out. Where a wall crosses the edge of a light's reach the corner is exact.
- **Pings and quick drawing** (owner's request, 2026-10-08): anyone who can see a map pings a spot (the Ping tool, or Alt+click with any tool) or sketches a line (the Draw tool, up to 500 points). Nothing is saved: it goes out live (`ping`, `draw` on the map's live stream) and fades after a few seconds. Colour: the sender's character token, gold for the DM. Players get the DM's, their own, and other players' only where they can see them. At most 20 in 10 seconds per person.
- **Planned movement and difficult terrain** (owner's request, 2026-10-08): while dragging a token, Space (or W) drops a waypoint, so a move can go round corners; walls are checked on every leg for players (token PATCH `path`). The label shows the distance the 5e way, "difficult" when it crosses difficult terrain (each square there costs double; `pathCost`), and in a fight how far the token has moved this turn against its speed (red when over; only shown, not enforced). Speed: the DM's number on the token, else the player's sheet, else the stat block (`move_speed`). In a fight each entry counts `moved`, reset when its turn starts. Difficult terrain (`terrain`: `{ id, points, source }`, up to 300) is dragged out by the DM as rectangles (snapped to squares) or suggested by the AI's wall draft (water, rubble, undergrowth); players see the parts they can see, hatched.
- **Other pictures of a map** (variants, owner's request, 2026-10-08): the DM adds more pictures of the same map in Map settings (night, after a fire, a secret door showing). Each upload is archived as it came (`variant-<id>-original.<ext>`) beside a copy stretched to the map's size (`variant-<id>.<ext>`), so tokens, walls and fog stay in place. A picker in the map bar switches which one everyone sees (`variant`, null for the original); players only learn the name of the one showing. The AI reads and drafts walls from the original.
- **Full screen** (owner's request, 2026-10-09): **Full screen** on the map toolbar (everyone; hideable under Look like the other general tools) puts the whole page full screen with the browser's Fullscreen API (so dialogs, dice and the panel beside the map still work) and marks it `data-map-full` on `<html>`. The top bar hides, the map fills the screen and the toolbars float over it, in their usual place from Look (top, bottom, or down the left; along the bottom on phones). Only the main tools show (owner, 2026-10-09): Fit, Measure, Ping, Draw, Template, Initiative and Exit full screen, and the DM's Show players this map and Edit (the buttons marked `map-main`), plus any tool that's switched on; **More** shows the rest (Grid, Walls, Pin, Beside the map, Sheet window, the DM's tools) after them, and they tuck away again when the map is touched, More (now Less) is pressed, or full screen ends. Each tool is see-through (opacity 0.55) and turns solid while the pointer is over the toolbars or one has keyboard focus; a tool that's switched on (Measure, Fog & sight, a wall mode) stays solid so you can tell. The turn order and the picked token's bar move clear of the toolbars. Where the browser can't go full screen (an iPhone, or it refuses), the map still fills the window. **Exit full screen** (the same button), Escape (not while a dialog or a field has it), the browser leaving full screen, or going to another tab puts the page back. All in the page (`web/public/map/full-screen.js`, CSS in `style.css`); nothing on the server.
- **Edit map** (DM; owner, 2026-10-09; reworked 2026-10-20: "we now have a bunch of map editing stuff even though we have a dedicated map editor… trim it down… without removing any actual features"): the play view has the play tools plus **Add token**, **Fog & sight**, the picture picker and **New map** (a dialog: Import a map, or Make a map in the creator). **Edit map** (also out in full screen) swaps them for the edit bar: Done, Map settings, Open in the map creator (made maps), Wall, Curve, Circle, Obstacle, Door, Lock, Light, Difficult terrain, Raised area (+ height, Bridge, Check AI heights, Accept all), Stairs, Ladder, Link, Place, Erase, AI walls…, Walls from file, Download .dd2vtt, Clear AI draft, and the counts. The map list, Grid, Fit, Measure and Full screen stay. Walls, lights, raised areas and stairs always show while editing; in play, Fog & sight's Show walls decides (`dndapp.map.showWalls`). Not kept between visits (it always opens on play). Imported pictures keep their walls drawn here, since the map creator only edits maps it made. Page only; the server's rules are unchanged.
  - **AI walls effort** (owner, 2026-10-20; first proposed in PR #33): **AI walls…** opens a dialog with Low, Medium, High, Extra high and Max, each with a line on what it means, the server's usual (`EFFORT_WALLS`, `xhigh`) marked and picked unless the DM picked another last time (kept per browser, `dndapp.map.wallsEffort`). `POST .../walls/draft` takes `{ effort? }` (one of `EFFORTS`; left out, the task's); the map keeps it in `wall_draft.effort` and the working and done messages say it. Try again reuses it. `GET .../maps` gives the DM `walls_effort`.
- **What's picked** (a token, a pin, a door, a template, a raised area; owner, 2026-10-20): its bar sits **below** the map in the page, not over it (in full screen and on a phone, where there's no page around the map, it floats over the map's bottom edge as narrow as it can). A token's bar is one short row: name, kind and size, hit points and the change box, conditions, Hidden, Shop/Roll/Stat block, Edit, and **More** (a menu that opens upwards: Light, Tall, Up, Under the bridge, Record, Stat block (AI), Picture, No picture, Save to creatures, Remove; Dismiss for a player's companion).
- **Add a token's Who is it?** (owner, 2026-10-20): **Kind** comes first; the list below a search box shows only those of that kind (Player character: the players; Enemy or NPC: the DM's creatures of that kind and the records' people filed as one, a record whose kind says monster, creature, enemy, villain, beast or foe counting as an enemy), with Someone new always first. Places and things from the records show only when searched for. The search matches anywhere in a name, any case; Enter picks the first match. Picking a saved creature goes to placing it, as before.
- **Links between maps** (owner's request, 2026-10-08): the DM places links (stairs, a door, a trapdoor) that lead to another map (`links`: `{ id, x, y, to, label }`, up to 50; the "Link" tool in Edit map). Clicking one sends the selected token (or a player's own character) through: players only from within a square and a half (`nearLink`), only to maps they're shown; the DM can send any token from anywhere, or just open the other map. The token arrives at the link on the other map that leads back, or its middle; between floors of the same building (same size, made in the creator) it arrives at the way back nearest the link it took, else the same spot. Things on a made map that lead to another map become its links (`source: 'creator'`, replaced when the design is saved again). Players see a link only where they can see it and when the other map is shown to them.
- **Map events for the archivist** (owner's request, 2026-10-08): when a session is processed, the archivist also gets what happened on the maps that day (`<map_events>`): maps shown, the DM bringing the players to a map, which picture, who appeared or was revealed, fights starting and ending, who went down or got back up (player characters with a sheet: through their sheet's changes, §5.2), conditions, doors opened, and characters going to another map, with the time. Worked out from the archived change lines (`maps/events.js`), so a rebuild gives the same; a change counts to a date the same way notes do (before the rollover hour it's the day before). Only maps players could see at the time, and never hidden tokens. The same thing several times in a row is said once; at most 300 lines.
- **Who can do what (server-enforced):** only the DM imports, renames, shows/hides, changes the grid, scale, fog or walls, re-reads, removes maps, and adds, edits or removes tokens. A player can only change a token tied to them (move it, its hit points and conditions), and only on a map they can see. Players see a map only when the DM has shown it, and never see the AI's description or reading notes (they could give away something the DM hasn't revealed), stat blocks, record links, hidden tokens, or anything under the fog. The admin login acts as DM in the API, as elsewhere.
- **Live:** `GET /campaigns/:cid/maps/events` (SSE) sends each change to everyone with the map open, filtered per viewer (a map being hidden is sent as `gone` to players).
- **Stored like sheets:** each map is one JSON document (`maps` table, schema v7) with a version. Every change appends only what changed to `maps/<id>/changes.jsonl` in the archive (the first line is the whole map), so restore replays it. Removing a map only marks it removed; nothing in the archive is deleted.
