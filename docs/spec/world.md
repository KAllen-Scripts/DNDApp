# The world: places, maps inside maps, travel (SPEC §6.6)

- **The world: places, and maps inside maps** (owner, 2026-10-18; research in the project's `research/world/connected-world-research.md`; first stage built 2026-10-20 on "Start"). Second stage (where everyone is) and third stage (travel and time) built 2026-10-21, each on "Start on it". No hex grid (owner: "No hex grid").
  - **Places** (`places` on a map, up to 200: `{ id, name, x, y, points?, group, to, record, revealed, revealed_to }`, `normalizePlaces` in `shared/src/map.js`): a pin, or an area (`points`, with x, y where its name goes). **Place** in Edit map: a click puts a place there, a drag makes an area; with Place on, a click on a place opens its settings and a drag moves it (an area moves whole); Erase removes one. The dialog has the name, a **group** (Towns, Roads…; the groups used on any map are suggested), the map it **opens** (any map that isn't already a place elsewhere and isn't around this one), who can see it, and the archivist's record about it if it has one (`record`, the DM's, opened from the dialog). `PATCH .../maps/:mid/places` `{ add?, set?, move?, remove? }` (DM).
  - **Maps inside maps:** a place that opens a map puts that map inside this one. Each map is a place on one map only, and a map can't be inside itself (`placeProblem`). A map's parent is the map holding the place that opens it (`parentOf`); `mapTrail` gives the maps from the top down, and `atlasOrder` the maps in world order (several top maps are allowed: other planes, a second continent). A click on a place opens its map (for anyone who has it); the **breadcrumb** over the top of the map (`#map-trail`, "The Vale › Millbrook › The Drowned Rat", only on a map inside another) goes back up; the map list puts each map under the map it's a place on (`↳`). Links stay for moving tokens between maps at the same scale.
  - **Groups of places:** a **Places** button beside the map list (when the map has groups) picks which groups show, on this screen only (`dndapp.map.hiddenGroups.<campaign>`).
  - **Who sees what** (owner, 2026-10-18): Map settings → **Who can see this map**: nobody (hidden), every player, or only the players ticked (`shown_to`: user ids, null for everyone; `shownTo(map, userId)`); a place likewise (`revealed`, `revealed_to`; `placeRevealedTo`), hidden until the DM reveals it. A player gets a map only if it's shown to them (the map list, the map, its picture, live updates: a player taken off the list gets `gone`), links only to maps shown to them, and places revealed to them where they can see the place's spot (or a corner of its area) under the fog; what a place opens only if that map is shown to them; never a place's record or who else it's revealed to, nor a map's `shown_to`. The DM's map list says "(shown to 2)" and the status line "shown to 2 players". Show players this map keeps whoever it's shown to, so it moves only those players.
  - **Where everyone is** (second stage, 2026-10-21; owner: "We don't want one token for the whole party, because they won't always all travel as one. We must handle that properly"): there's no party token; each character travels on their own or with others.
    - **One map per character:** a player character's token is on one map at a time. When the DM adds a character on a map (Add token, or the party panel's Bring here), their token comes over from the map they're on (the same token: hit points, conditions and all) with whoever follows it; one already on this map just moves; a token turned into a character who's on a map already is refused ("Bring them here from the party panel instead"). Any other copy of a character on a map (from before this) goes the next time they move between maps (`characterMap` in `shared/src/map.js` picks the map changed last meanwhile).
    - **Following** (`follows` on a token: the id of a token on the same map, or null; `followGroup`, `followLoop`, `trailFollowers`): the picked token's bar has **Follows…** (the DM for any token; a player for their own character and companions, following any token they can see; never round in a circle). When the leader moves, its followers walk behind it along the same way, one behind the other (not in a fight, where everyone moves on their own turn); moving a follower yourself stops it following. Whoever follows goes along through links, into places and out again, and is brought over with the leader, arriving round the spot (`arrivalSpots`) still following. A player's new companion or summon follows their character (owner, 2026-10-14's "companions kept so they follow the character between maps"). Players get `follows` only when they can see the token followed.
    - **Into a place and out:** a token standing at a place that opens a map (inside its area, or as near its pin as next to a link; `nearPlace`) gets **Go into <place>**: it and its followers go to that map, at its way back out (a link to this map) or its middle. On a map inside another, **Out to <map>** takes them out to this map's place there. Players take their own, into places revealed to them that open maps they're shown; out only to a map they're shown, standing at a way out if the map has one they can see (anywhere if it has none). `POST .../maps/:mid/places/:pid/enter`, `POST .../maps/:mid/leave` `{ token }`.
    - **Pins on world and region maps** (`isPinMap`: a map whose kind is world or region; Map settings now has **Kind**, and a kind the DM picks stays when the AI reads the map again, `kind_set`): tokens are round pins 40 px across on screen at any zoom, with their picture or initials and name; pins that would cover each other at this zoom fan out in a ring round their spot (`pinFan` in `web/public/map/world.js`), worked out again when the zoom settles.
    - **A player's page follows their character:** it opens on the map their character is on (else the map the DM brought everyone to), and goes there, saying "Thorin is here now", whenever their character turns up on another map. They can look elsewhere meanwhile. Show players this map works as before.
    - **The party panel** (DM, **Party** on the map toolbar): every player's character, the map they're on, whom they follow (and any other map they're also on, from before one map per character), with **Go there** and **Bring here** (the middle of what's on screen).
  - **Travel and time** (third stage, 2026-10-21; owner: "an in-game clock is part of the basics"; journeys "with their own arrival times"):
    - **The in-game clock** (`clock` in the campaign's settings: minutes since day 1 began, starting at Day 1, 08:00; `clockText` "Day 14, 18:30", or the campaign's calendar's date (§6.11, stage 4a), `durationText` in `shared/src/settings.js`). It shows over the map for everyone; the DM's opens **The in-game clock**: +1 hour, +8 hours, +1 day, **To the next arrival**, or a day and time to set, with everyone on the road listed. A rest (Rest) moves it on an hour or 8 hours when "Move the in-game clock on" is ticked (the default). `GET /campaigns/:cid/clock`; `POST /campaigns/:cid/clock` `{ add?, set?, next_arrival? }` (DM); the map list has `clock`, and the maps' live stream sends `clock`.
    - **Journeys** (`journey` on a token: `{ from, to, leaves, arrives, pace, miles, to_name }`, `normalizeJourney`): on a world or region map, the picked token's **Travel…** (the DM for any token, a player for their own) asks where to (a place on the map, or a spot clicked on it) and the pace: slow, normal or fast, 18, 24 or 30 miles a day (both editions' tables), with each edition's effect said (2014: fast −5 passive Perception, slow can sneak; 2024: disadvantage or advantage on Perception and Survival). The miles come from the map's scale (`milesBetween`, any unit); the time from the miles a day, 8 hours on the road a day (`travelMinutes`: 30 miles at a normal pace is a day, a night and 2 hours). The DM can type the days and hours instead (the only way on a map with no scale). It and whoever follows it set off by the clock; as the clock moves they move along a straight line (`journeyAt`), and when it reaches their time they're there. A dashed line runs from each to where it's headed. On the road, the bar says where to and when they're due, with **Stop here** (stops where they are now) and the DM's **Arrive now**. Moving a token by hand, or taking it through a link or a place, ends its journey. `POST .../maps/:mid/tokens/:tid/journey` `{ x, y } | { place }, pace?, minutes? (DM) | { stop: true } | { arrive: true } (DM)`. Players see where another token is going only as a line, not the place's name.
    - The **party panel** says who's on the road and when they're due.
    - **Map events** say who set off (together, in one line), where to, how far, at what pace, and when they left and are due by the in-game clock, and who arrived (with the in-game time) or stopped on the way; the archivist's instructions say to use the in-game day for dates (`PIPELINE_VERSION` 24).
    - Not yet: routes along roads and rivers, difficult terrain slowing a journey, forced marches, mounts and vehicles, merchants restocking by days instead of long rests (all built in the fourth stage, below).
  - **More to travel** (fourth stage, 2026-10-21; owner: "Also wagons and maybe even random encounters as we travel"; "A lot of these should also be optional. Something the DM can toggle on and off. For example, some DM's might want to timeskip past travel. Not all tables want the same level of detail and realism"):
    - **The table's choice** (`travel` in the campaign's settings, Campaign settings → Travel in the world; `normalizeTravel` in `shared/src/settings.js`): **Travel time** is `clock` (journeys take their time as the DM moves the clock on; the default), `skip` (they get there at once and the clock jumps on by the journey) or `none` (they get there at once and the clock stays put); and switches for **roads** and rivers, difficult **terrain**, **vehicles** (off: everyone walks), forced **marches** and random **encounters**, all on by default. `PATCH /campaigns/:cid/settings` takes `travel` with any of them; the rest stay.
    - **Roads and rivers** (`roads` on a map: stretches `{ id, kind: road | river, x1, y1, x2, y2 }`, up to 2000, `normalizeRoads`): the DM's **Road** and **River** tools in Edit map (world and region maps only) drag a stretch at a time, snapping to the end of another (`snapRoadPoint`); Erase removes one. They show only to the DM while editing; players never get them (the server works out the way). `PATCH .../maps/:mid/roads` `{ add?, remove?, clear?: 'all' }`.
    - **The way a journey goes** (`planJourney` in `server/src/maps/travel.js`): over land, straight across, or along the roads (stretches whose ends meet, or an end on another stretch, join into a network; the shortest way along it between the points nearest each end) when that's quicker; on a road difficult terrain doesn't count. Off the roads, the miles inside the map's difficult terrain count double. Boats keep to the rivers the same way. The journey keeps the way (`route`: points with how far along in time each is), the token moves along it, and the dashed line follows it.
    - **How they travel** (`TRAVEL_BY` in `shared/src/map.js`; `by` on a journey): on foot; riding (the same miles a day, the books say; a mount can gallop for an hour); a wagon or cart (off the roads everything is as slow as difficult terrain: our call, the books say nothing); a rowboat (1½ mph, 8 hours a day), keelboat (1), sailing ship (2), longship (3), galley (4) or warship (2½), crewed boats going day and night (the PHB's vehicles table). Boats have no pace. Followers go the same way (they ride along).
    - **Hours a day** (`hours` on a journey, 1 to 24; 8 on land, 24 for crewed boats): time is worked out with that many hours on the road a day (`travelMinutes`), and tokens move only while on the road (`journeyAt`: camped the rest of the day). Past 8 on land is a **forced march**: the travel dialog warns, and as the clock passes each such day the DM's notes say who must make Constitution saves at DC 11, 12… (one per hour past 8) or gain a level of exhaustion.
    - **The travel dialog** asks the server what the trip would be (`{ preview: true }` → `{ plan: { miles, road_miles, rough_miles, minutes, arrives, route, by, hours, time } }`) and says it: "60 miles (60 by road): 1 day 10 hours at a normal pace, arriving …", or that it's skipped. `POST .../tokens/:tid/journey` takes `by` and `hours`.
    - **Random encounters** (`encounters` on a world or region map, in Map settings, and on an area place there, in its dialog: `{ chance (in 100), every (hours), table: [lines] }`, `normalizeEncounters`; the DM's alone): as the clock moves forward, each group on the road (or camped) is checked every so many hours since it set off; an area's own table counts inside it instead of the map's. A hit picks a line and rolls any dice in it ("1d6+2 wolves" → "5 (1d6+2) wolves", the server's dice). With "Stop for random encounters" (the clock dialog, on by default) the clock stops at the first one, so everything is where it was then; a check isn't rolled twice (`noted` on a journey). Rests that move the clock do the same. The DM's notes (`notes` in the reply to `POST /campaigns/:cid/clock`, to a rest, and to a skipped journey) list encounters, forced marches and restocks with the in-game time; players never see them, and they don't go to the archivist (the DM runs what happens).
    - **Merchants restock by days** (`restock.days` and `restock.clock`; the merchant's "Or every … in-game days"): once that many days of the clock have gone by since the last restock (counted from when the DM set it), the clock restocks them. Restock now starts the count again.
    - **Map events** say how they travelled, how much was by road or river and whether they pushed on (`PIPELINE_VERSION` 25).
    - Not yet: weather, getting lost (navigation checks), a mount's gallop, wagons' cargo, food and water, encounters by time of day.
  - **Map events** on a map shown to only some players end with "[seen only by user 3, user 5]", and the archivist's instructions say only they (and the DM) know it; a map shown to nobody in its list counts as hidden (`PIPELINE_VERSION` 21).
